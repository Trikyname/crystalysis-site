Long

Crystalysis is a slingshot roguelite built on one rule: while you pull back to aim, the world holds its breath. Time only moves when your ball is in flight.

You defend a single crystal at the heart of the arena from endless waves closing in from every side. Your ball is immortal, but it slows down — and damage is speed. Slingshot it, ricochet it off the walls, catch it mid-flight to re-aim, and fight to keep it fast. Let it crawl and the crystal starts to bleed.

Between waves you draft weapons, passives and evolutions in the moment, survivors-style. Prove you have mastered a trick and the crystal permanently unlocks the next one: an arsenal earned by playing, not by grinding.

It plays in portrait with one thumb, works fully offline, and speaks thirteen languages.

What makes it different

  1. Aiming freezes the world. Not a pause and not bullet time — it is the game's simulation rule. You can think about a shot for as long as you like, in a genre built on constant pressure. What a shot costs you is energy, not time.
  2. One ball, not an army. All your damage goes through a single ricocheting projectile that you aim yourself. Damage is proportional to its speed, so a run is a fight to keep it moving.
  3. The crystal teaches the ball. Permanent unlocks are earned by demonstrating that you can use something, never by accumulating currency.
  4. A daily run identical for everyone on earth. Same map, same weapons, same seed. The only variable is how you play it, and it has its own leaderboard.
  5. Deliberate minimalism. Vector art, colour that carries mechanical meaning, and audio that is 100% synthesised by the game itself — there is not a single audio sample in the build.

The developer

Crystalysis is made by one person in Spain. It is self-funded, has no publisher and no revenue to date. It started as a prototype to answer a single design question — what happens to a horde survival game if the world stops while you aim? — and kept going because the answer turned out to be interesting.

The whole thing is built in Unity, including the parts that would normally be bought in: every sound in the game is generated procedurally at runtime, and the store artwork is rendered by the game's own UI code so it can never drift from the build it advertises.

Un párrafo

Crystalysis es un roguelite de tirachinas construido sobre una regla: mientras estiras para apuntar, el mundo contiene la respiración. El tiempo solo avanza cuando tu bola está en vuelo. Defiendes un único cristal de oleadas infinitas con una bola inmortal que nunca muere pero sí se frena — y el daño es la velocidad. Entre oleadas eliges armas, pasivas y evoluciones al estilo survivors, y el cristal desbloquea arsenal nuevo de forma permanente según demuestras que sabes usarlo.

Qué lo distingue

  1. Apuntar congela el mundo. No es una pausa ni bala tiempo: es la regla de simulación del juego. Puedes pensar el tiro con calma en un género que vive de la presión constante. Lo que cuesta un tiro es energía, no tiempo.
  2. Una sola bola, no un ejército. Todo el daño pasa por un proyectil que rebota y que diriges tú. El daño es proporcional a su velocidad, así que la partida es una lucha por no dejar que se frene.
  3. El cristal enseña a la bola. Los desbloqueos permanentes se ganan demostrando que sabes usar algo, nunca acumulando moneda.
  4. Una diaria idéntica para todo el mundo. Mismo mapa, mismas armas, misma semilla. Lo único que cambia es cómo la juegas, y tiene clasificación propia.
  5. Minimalismo deliberado. Arte vectorial, color con significado mecánico y sonido cien por cien sintetizado por el propio juego — no hay un solo sample en la build.
